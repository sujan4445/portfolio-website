Abstract (short)

Penicillium digitatum is a major postharvest pathogen causing green mould in citrus. Due to health risks and chemical resistance associated with synthetic fungicides, this study evaluated the in-vivo efficacy of three botanical extracts — chilli, garlic, and ginger — each at 5% and 15% concentrations. Pathogen growth was recorded at 3, 6 and 9 days after inoculation. All treatments significantly reduced green mould compared with the control (p ≤ 0.001). Chilli 15% produced the highest inhibition (46.72%), followed by garlic 15% (35.25%). Ginger showed the least effect (PI ≈ 6–8%). Chilli 15% also recorded the lowest disease severity and PDI among treatments. These botanicals show promise as eco-friendly postharvest measures.

Materials & Methods (detailed)

  • Experimental Design: Completely Randomized Design (CRD) with 3 replications per treatment.
  • Treatments:
    • Chilli extract at 5% and 15% concentration
    • Garlic extract at 5% and 15% concentration
    • Ginger extract at 5% and 15% concentration
    • Control: Distilled water application
  • Plant Material: Fresh, healthy mandarin fruits (Citrus reticulata) of uniform size and maturity.
  • Pathogen: Penicillium digitatum isolated from infected citrus fruits, maintained on PDA medium.
  • Inoculum Preparation: Spore suspension adjusted to 1 × 106 spores ml-1 using hemocytometer.
  • Extract Preparation: Aqueous extracts prepared by macerating 50g and 150g of each botanical in 1L distilled water, filtered through muslin cloth.
  • Application: Fruits were surface sterilized, wounded (3mm deep), treated with extracts, then inoculated with pathogen.
  • Observation Parameters: Lesion diameter measurement and disease scoring at 3, 6, and 9 days after inoculation.
  • Data Analysis: Calculated Percent Disease Index (PDI), Disease Severity (DS), and Percent Inhibition (PI). Statistical analysis using ANOVA and Duncan's Multiple Range Test (p ≤ 0.05).

Disease severity & lesion development

Across 9 days of observation, control fruits developed severe lesions reaching 100% severity. Chilli 15% significantly reduced lesion progression; at day 9 chilli 15% recorded ~53.28% severity while garlic 15% was ~64.60%. Ginger treatments produced marginal reduction versus control (≈92–95%).

Statistical Analysis

The data were subjected to analysis of variance (ANOVA) using statistical software, and means were separated using Duncan's Multiple Range Test at p ≤ 0.05.

Key Statistical Findings

  • All botanical extract treatments showed statistically significant differences from the control (p ≤ 0.001).
  • Chilli 15% was significantly superior to all other treatments in reducing disease severity and PDI.
  • Garlic 15% showed intermediate efficacy, significantly different from both chilli treatments and ginger treatments.
  • Ginger extracts at both concentrations were not significantly different from each other but were significantly different from control and other treatments.
  • Concentration effect was significant for chilli and garlic extracts, with 15% concentration performing significantly better than 5%.

Correlation Analysis

Strong negative correlation was observed between extract concentration and disease parameters for chilli (r = -0.89) and garlic (r = -0.76), indicating that higher concentrations provided better disease control.

Conclusion & Recommendations

Chilli and garlic extracts at 15% concentration showed meaningful inhibition of P. digitatum in vivo and reduced disease severity and PDI compared to the control. Ginger aqueous extracts provided minimal control under these conditions.

Key Conclusions

  • Chilli extract at 15% concentration demonstrated the highest antifungal activity against P. digitatum.
  • Garlic extract at 15% concentration showed moderate but significant antifungal properties.
  • Ginger aqueous extracts exhibited minimal antifungal activity against citrus green mould.
  • Higher concentrations (15%) were more effective than lower concentrations (5%) for both chilli and garlic extracts.
  • Botanical extracts offer a promising eco-friendly alternative to synthetic fungicides for postharvest disease management in citrus.

Recommendations for Future Research

  • Investigate alcoholic (ethanolic or methanolic) extracts which may yield higher concentrations of active compounds.
  • Test a broader concentration range (5-25%) to determine optimal application rates.
  • Evaluate combination treatments of different botanicals for synergistic effects.
  • Conduct shelf-life studies to determine the persistence of antifungal activity during storage.
  • Investigate the mode of action of effective botanical extracts at cellular and molecular levels.
  • Perform field trials to validate laboratory findings under commercial conditions.
