UAV-Based Precision Nitrogen Management in Rice

Multispectral remote sensing for site-specific fertilizer recommendations

Background

Precision nitrogen management is critical for improving rice productivity while reducing excessive fertilizer use and environmental pollution. Conventional blanket fertilizer recommendations fail to account for within-field variability in crop nitrogen status.

Rice fields often exhibit significant spatial variability in soil fertility, topography, and plant growth patterns, which can lead to both under- and over-application of nitrogen. This not only affects crop yield but also increases the risk of nitrate leaching into groundwater and emissions of nitrous oxide, a potent greenhouse gas.

Recent advances in unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and multispectral imaging provide an opportunity to capture high-resolution data across entire fields rapidly and efficiently. Vegetation indices derived from UAV imagery, such as NDVI, NDRE, and GNDVI, can serve as proxies for plant nitrogen content, chlorophyll levels, and biomass.

Integrating these remote sensing indicators with ground-based measurements enables precise, site-specific nitrogen recommendations. The adoption of UAV-based nitrogen management can optimize input use efficiency, reduce production costs, and minimize environmental impacts. Additionally, it provides a foundation for scalable digital agriculture platforms that can be deployed in diverse agroecological settings.

Objective

To develop a UAV-based, site-specific nitrogen management approach using multispectral imagery and field measurements, supporting a scalable decision support system for rice farmers.

Materials and Methods

A DJI Phantom 4 RTK multispectral UAV was deployed at key rice growth stages to capture high-resolution imagery. Vegetation indices such as NDVI, NDRE, and GNDVI were generated to assess crop health, biomass, and nitrogen status.

UAV-derived indices were correlated with ground-based observations including SPAD chlorophyll readings, measured chlorophyll content, and final grain yield. Spatial maps were produced to identify nutrient-deficient zones and guide variable-rate fertilizer application.
