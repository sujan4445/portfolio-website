Screening Wheat Genotypes Against Leaf Rust (Puccinia triticina) in Open Field Conditions

Leaf rust pustule severity and response scoring under open field conditions
Field evaluation of disease progression across diverse wheat accessions
Genotype 'Chyakhura' exhibiting low severity and slow-rusting response

  • Co-Researchers: Research Team & Plant Pathology Collaborators
  • Target Pathogen: Puccinia triticina Erikss.
  • Publication Status: Under Revision — Australasian Plant Pathology

Background

Leaf rust, caused by the fungal pathogen Puccinia triticina, represents one of the most widespread and damaging foliar diseases of bread wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) globally and regionally in Nepal. Under favorable weather conditions, severe epidemics can destroy functional green leaf area during critical grain-filling stages, resulting in substantial yield loss. While chemical control is available, deployment of genetic resistance—particularly Adult Plant Resistance (APR) or "slow-rusting" capacity—provides an environmentally friendly and economically viable strategy. Evaluating epidemiological metrics under natural epiphytotic field conditions is essential to differentiate completely susceptible genotypes from those possessing durable field resistance.

Objectives

  • To systematically screen commercial cultivars, advanced breeding lines, and local landraces against leaf rust under open field epiphytotic conditions.
  • To calculate key disease parameters including Area Under Disease Progress Curve (AUDPC), Relative AUDPC (rAUDPC), and Coefficient of Infection (CI).
  • To estimate the infection rate (r-value) and disease progress over time across multiple developmental stages.
  • To identify promising genotypes exhibiting High Adult Plant Resistance (APR) and slow-rusting characteristics for integration into elite breeding lines.

Methodology & Field Work

The field experiment was conducted using an Alpha Lattice / Randomized Complete Block Design across multiple scoring intervals post-disease initiation. Disease severity was visually assessed using the modified Cobb scale, combined with host reaction categories ranging from Immune (I), Resistant (R), Moderately Resistant (MR), Moderately Susceptible (MS), to Susceptible (S). Quantitative epidemiological assessments included calculating CI by combining disease severity with reaction constants, as well as computing AUDPC to measure cumulative disease intensity over time. Apparent infection rates (r) were determined using logistic transformation models.

Key Outcomes & Findings

  • Identification of Resistant Genotypes: The genotype Chyakhura demonstrated outstanding field performance, recording the lowest AUDPC and minimal Coefficient of Infection (CI) values among tested accessions.
  • Slow-Rusting Trait Validation: Low infection rates (r-value) and reduced rAUDPC confirmed the presence of Adult Plant Resistance (APR) mechanisms in high-performing lines including Chyakhura.
  • Efficacy of Quantitative Parameters: AUDPC and CI proved to be highly reliable selection criteria for categorizing partial/slow-rusting resistance versus highly susceptible lines.
  • Breeding Utility: Identified lines with low AUDPC and partial resistance serve as crucial genetic donors for durable rust resistance breeding programs in Nepal and similar agro-ecological zones.
