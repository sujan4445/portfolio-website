Background

Nepal harbors significant finger millet (Eleusine coracana L.) landrace diversity adapted to hilly and mountain agro-ecologies. Finger millet landraces in Nepal are a vital part of the country’s agricultural heritage, valued for their resilience to harsh climates and poor soils. They provide essential nutrients, supporting food security and healthy diets in rural communities. Beyond nutrition, these landraces hold deep cultural significance, forming the basis of traditional foods and rituals. They also represent rich genetic diversity, which is crucial for breeding, adaptation, and long-term crop resilience. However, many of these landraces are now on the verge of extinction due to modern agricultural practices and shifting cropping patterns. Their characterization and documentation have become essential for ensuring their conservation and promoting agricultural sustainability.

Objectives

  • Characterize landrace diversity using key agro-morphological traits.
  • Assess genetic variability, heritability, and clustering patterns among indigenous landraces.
  • Identify superior landraces using multi-trait selection indices for crop improvement.
  • Promote on-farm conservation and sustainable utilization of local genetic resources.

Design & Methodology

The experiment was conducted using a Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) with three replications. A total of 18 landraces collected from the mid-hills of Nepal along with one released check variety, Kavre Kodo-2, were evaluated in the field. Assessment of 29 qualitative traits based on standardized descriptors from the International Board for Plant Genetic Resources (IBPGR) and the International Union for the Protection of New Varieties of Plants (UPOV). Statistical evaluation included Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), frequency distribution, Chi-square test, Correlation Analysis, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Cluster Analysis, and the Multi-Trait Genotype-Ideotype Distance Index (MGIDI).

Outcomes & Significance

  • Comprehensive baseline documentation of morphological and phenological traits of Nepalese finger millet landraces.
  • Identification of trait associations and genetic parameters essential for direct selection in yield improvement.
  • Selection of superior landraces utilizing MGIDI index for integration into breeding pipelines.
  • Strengthened framework for on-farm genetic conservation in mid-hill agro-ecosystems.
