Agro-Morphological Diversity in Finger Millet

Characterization, variability analysis, and conservation of landraces

Description

Nepal harbors significant finger millet (Eleusine coracana) landrace diversity adapted to hilly and mountain agro-ecologies. This project characterizes landraces for genetic, phenotypic, and agronomic variability to inform selection and on-farm conservation. Agro-morphological traits such as PH, DM, EL, TGW, and GY were evaluated, alongside heritability, genetic advance, and cluster analysis.

Background

Finger millet is a climate-resilient cereal crop with high nutritional value, widely grown in Nepal's hilly regions.

Objectives

  • Characterize landrace diversity using agro-morphological traits.
  • Assess variability and clustering among landraces.
  • Promote on-farm conservation of local landraces.

Design / Method

Field evaluation of 20 landraces under spring season; measurement of plant height, days to maturity, ear length, 1000-grain weight, grain yield; data analyzed using ANOVA, PCA, and clustering.

Funding & Timeline
