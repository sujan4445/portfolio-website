Agro-Morphological Diversity in Finger Millet

Characterization, variability analysis, and conservation of landraces

Project Details

  • Funding Agency: Nepal Academy of Science and Technology (NAST)
  • Principal Investigator: Sujan Chapagain
  • Project Supervisors: Ankur Poudel, Bishnu Prasad Kandel and Sudikshya Shrestha

Description

Nepal harbors significant finger millet (Eleusine coracana) landrace diversity adapted to hilly and mountain agro-ecologies. This project characterizes landraces for genetic, phenotypic, and agronomic variability to inform selection and on-farm conservation. Agro-morphological traits such as PH, DM, EL, TGW, and GY were evaluated, alongside heritability, genetic advance, and cluster analysis.

Background

Finger millet landraces in Nepal are a vital part of the country’s agricultural heritage, valued for their resilience to harsh climates and poor soils. They provide essential nutrients, supporting food security and healthy diets in rural communities.

Beyond nutrition, these landraces hold deep cultural significance, forming the basis of traditional foods and rituals. They also represent rich genetic diversity, which is crucial for breeding, adaptation, and long-term crop resilience.

However, many of these landraces are now on the verge of extinction due to modern agricultural practices and shifting cropping patterns. Their characterization and documentation have become essential for ensuring their conservation.

Promoting their cultivation contributes to agricultural sustainability, climate resilience, and the preservation of Nepal’s cultural and genetic heritage.

Objectives

  • Characterize landrace diversity using agro-morphological traits.
  • Assess variability and clustering among landraces.
  • Promote on-farm conservation of local landraces.

Design / Method

The experiment was conducted using a Randomized Complete Block Design (RCBD) with three replications. A total of 18 landraces collected from the mid-hills of Nepal and one released check variety, Kavre Kodo-2, were evaluated in the field.

Morpho-phenological Diversity:

  • Assessment of 29 qualitative traits based on descriptors from the International Board for Plant Genetic Resources (IBPGR) and the International Union for the Protection of New Varieties of Plants (UPOV).

Quantitative Traits Recorded:

  • Plant Height
  • Effective Tillers
  • Flag Leaf Area
  • Days to Flowering
  • Days to Maturity
  • Ear Length
  • Finger Width
  • 1000-Grain Weight
  • Biological Yield
  • Harvest Index (%)
  • Grain Yield

Data Analysis: Frequency distribution (Pie Charts), Chi-square test, ANOVA, Correlation Analysis, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and Cluster Analysis were performed.
