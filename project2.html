In vivo Efficacy of Botanical Extracts Against Penicillium digitatum (Pers.: Fr.) Sacc.

Evaluating low-cost, sustainable eco-friendly bio-fungicides to control postharvest green mould in Nepalese citrus

Background

Citrus is a key horticultural crop in Nepal, cultivated across 64 districts. However, post-harvest losses remain exceedingly high, affecting 30–50% of total production. Fungal pathogens, particularly Penicillium digitatum, cause green mould—the most economically damaging postharvest disease of citrus worldwide—leading to rapid fruit decay, shrinkage, and mycotoxin contamination. While synthetic chemical fungicides represent the conventional management approach, concerns regarding chemical residues, environmental safety, worker health, and fungicide resistance necessitate safe, biodegradable alternatives. Botanical extracts possess natural antifungal and antioxidant bioactive compounds; however, their in vivo efficacy against P. digitatum on Nepalese citrus mandarins remains largely unexamined.

Objective

To evaluate the in vivo antifungal efficacy of crude botanical extracts against green mould (Penicillium digitatum) on citrus fruits, providing a low-cost, sustainable, and eco-friendly postharvest strategy for smallholder citrus producers.

Materials & Methods

Fresh mandarin fruits were surface-sterilized and immersed in prepared aqueous botanical extracts (chilli, garlic, and ginger) at designated concentrations for 3 minutes. Treated fruits were artificially wounded and inoculated with a calibrated spore suspension of P. digitatum (1 × 106 spores/mL). Inoculated fruits were stored under controlled environment conditions (25 ± 2°C, >85% RH). Disease incidence, severity, Percent Disease Index (PDI), and percent growth inhibition were recorded at 3, 6, and 9 days post-inoculation (DPI).

Results & Key Findings

  • Disease Severity Reduction: Disease severity was significantly lower in fruits treated with 15% chilli extract (53.28%) compared to untreated controls (100%). Garlic extract also demonstrated significant disease reduction.
  • Percent Disease Index (PDI): The lowest PDI was recorded under 15% chilli extract (58.33%), followed by 15% garlic extract, whereas ginger extracts showed minimal therapeutic efficacy.
  • Mycelial Growth Inhibition: Maximum growth inhibition was achieved by 15% chilli extract (46.72%) and 15% garlic extract (35.25%), compared to ginger extracts (6.0–7.8%).

Major Finding: Crude 15% chilli extract demonstrated superior bio-fungicidal efficacy, suppressing green mould decay by 46.72%, followed by 15% garlic extract at 35.25%. Active bioactive constituents such as capsaicinoids (chilli) and allicin (garlic) serve as prime candidate compounds for natural postharvest formulations.

Future Prospects

The demonstrated antifungal activity opens valuable avenues for non-chemical postharvest management. Future research will focus on evaluating these botanical treatments under real-world farm storage conditions, assessing their effect on fruit organoleptic and sensory qualities, exploring synergistic combinations with biological control agents, and conducting cost-benefit analyses for rural farming communities.
