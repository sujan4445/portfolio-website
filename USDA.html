Impact of Cover Crops on Plant Yield, Insect Pests, Weeds and Soil Health in Organic Vegetable Production

Research Project Assistant · Tanahu, Nepal

Funded By: USDA-FAS-Tennessee State University

Project Description

This two-year collaborative study evaluated the impact of cover crops on yield, insect pests, weeds, and soil health in organic vegetable and rice production across Nepal. The project was conducted in partnership with IAAS TU and Tennessee State University.

Key Activities

  • Collected field data on cabbage growth, plant vigor, and agro-morphological parameters under organic management.
  • Monitored soil temperature, tracked nutrient dynamics, and measured soil organic matter across treatments.
  • Maintained field sampling schedules and experimental trial records throughout the project duration.

Key Findings

  • Observed a 24% higher cabbage yield under cover crop treatments compared to control plots.
  • Mung bean integration improved soil nitrogen levels and overall organic matter.
  • Cooler soil conditions enhanced crop heat resilience during peak temperatures.
  • Integrated Sesbania and cowpea cover crops significantly boosted long-term soil fertility and rotational crop yields.
