Effect of Botanical Treatments and Oxalic Acid on Shelf Life and Quality of Mandarin

Postharvest botanical approaches to reduce spoilage and fungal decay in Nepalese mandarin

Background

Mandarin, Nepal’s national fruit, contributes significantly to the national fruit economy but experiences 15–20% postharvest losses due to fungal decay and poor handling. This study was conducted at the Plant Pathology Laboratory, Lamjung Campus, to evaluate the effectiveness of botanical extracts and oxalic acid in extending shelf life and maintaining fruit quality.

Research Info

Department: Horticulture & Crop Protection

Supervisor: Asst. Prof. Dipendra Rana

Institution: Lamjung Campus, IAAS

Duration: June 6–22, 2025

Major Work Done

Preparation and standardization of neem, aloe vera, garlic, ginger, citronella extracts, and oxalic acid treatments. Measurement of fruit quality parameters including pH, titratable acidity, total soluble solids (TSS), and fruit-to-juice ratio.

Materials and Methods

The experiment followed a single-factor RCBD with eight treatments replicated three times. Parameters assessed included physiological weight loss, firmness, decay loss, pH, TSS, TA, TSS:TA ratio, and consumer preference.

Results

Garlic extract (10%) recorded the lowest physiological weight loss, while Aloe vera extract maintained superior TSS:TA and fruit-to-juice ratio. Both treatments significantly reduced decay loss.

Key Finding: Aloe vera and garlic extracts significantly improved shelf life and fruit quality, offering eco-friendly postharvest solutions.

Conclusion

Locally available botanical extracts provide effective and sustainable alternatives to synthetic fungicides for postharvest disease management of mandarin in Nepal.
