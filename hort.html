Effect of Botanical Treatments and Oxalic Acid on Shelf Life and Quality of Mandarin

In vivo evaluation of postharvest botanicals to reduce spoilage and fungal decay in Nepalese mandarins

Background

Mandarin, Nepal’s national fruit, plays a major role in the country’s fruit industry but suffers 15–20% post-harvest losses due to fungal diseases and poor handling. To address this, an experiment was conducted at the Horticulture Lab of Lamjung Campus, Nepal, to evaluate botanical extracts and oxalic acid in extending mandarin shelf life, maintaining quality, and reducing postharvest spoilage.

Materials & Methods

The study followed a single-factor Randomized Completely Block Design (RCBD) with eight treatments (control, hot water 50°C, eucalyptus oil 1000 ppm, citronella oil 1000 ppm, oxalic acid 5%, garlic extract 10%, neem leaf extract 10%, and Aloe vera extract 10%) replicated three times. Parameters assessed included physiological weight loss (PWL), TSS, TA, firmness, decay loss, pH, fruit-to-juice ratio, TSS:TA, and consumer preference.

Results

Garlic extract (10%) resulted in the lowest physiological weight loss (3.19–2.84% across storage days), while Aloe vera extract maintained the highest TSS:TA and fruit-to-juice ratio. Decay loss was lowest in Aloe vera and garlic treatments, indicating strong antifungal and preservative properties.

Key Finding: Pre-dipping in Aloe vera and garlic extracts significantly improved mandarin shelf life, fruit quality, and disease control compared to other treatments.

Conclusion

This study highlights the potential of locally available botanical extracts as eco-friendly postharvest alternatives to synthetic fungicides. Aloe vera and garlic dips effectively reduced spoilage and quality deterioration, supporting sustainable citrus handling practices in Nepal.
