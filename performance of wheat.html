Performance evaluation and assessment of NDVI and canopy temperature in wheat under water stress

Characterization and performance evaluation under contrasting environments

Background

Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is a key staple in Nepal, crucial for food security and rural livelihoods. Most research focuses on the Terai, while the mid-hill region, largely rainfed and prone to moisture stress, has limited suitable varieties.

This study evaluated landraces, genotypes, and released varieties under contrasting environments, including two new genotypes, AFU W01 and AFU W02, developed by Professor Madhav Ghimire.

Enhancing wheat with high adaptability, yield stability, and drought tolerance can strengthen food security and resilience in the mid-hills.

Objectives

  • Assess performance of local and improved wheat genotypes.
  • Identify genotypes suitable for contrasting environments.
  • Support wheat breeding programs and future variety release.

Methods

A field experiment was conducted using an Alpha Lattice Design under two environmental conditions: Irrigated and Drought-stressed, with two replications per environment.

Data Collection:

  • Phenological Traits: Days to Booting, Days to Heading, Days to Anthesis, and Days to Maturity.
  • Yield-related Traits: Spike Length, Number of Spikelets, Number of Grains per Spike, Harvest Index, Biological Yield, and Grain Yield.
  • Physiological & Remote Sensing Traits: NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index), Canopy Temperature, FV/FM, FV/FO, and advanced OJIP measurements using a Chlorophyll Fluorometer.

Data Analysis: ANOVA, Post Hoc Tests, AMMI, GGE Biplot, PCA, and Cluster Analysis were conducted.
