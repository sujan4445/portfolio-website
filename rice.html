Diversity Assessment and Drought Screening of Nepalese Rice Landraces

Screening rice genotypes for drought tolerance and agro-morphological characterization

Project Details

  • Funding Agency: Nepal Academy of Science and Technology (NAST)
  • Researcher: Sujan Chapagain, Sangita Chapagain, Itsa Bogati and Sonia K.C.
  • Project Supervisors: Ankur Poudel, Bishnu Prasad Kandel

Description

Nepalese rice landraces are highly adapted to diverse agro-ecologies and drought-prone environments. This project focuses on evaluating genotypes for drought tolerance under controlled water stress and characterizing their agro-morphological diversity to inform breeding and conservation.

Background

Rice is a staple crop in Nepal, yet drought stress significantly limits yield in upland and rainfed areas. Local landraces hold unique genetic traits for adaptation, resilience, and yield stability. Agro-morphological characterization and drought screening are essential to conserve genetic diversity and select superior genotypes.

Objectives

  • Screen rice landraces for drought tolerance under CRD conditions.
  • Characterize agro-morphological diversity using Row–Row design.
  • Identify promising genotypes for breeding and on-farm conservation.

Methods

Drought Screening: Conducted using CRD with two moisture regimes: Well-watered (70% field capacity) and Water-stressed (35% field capacity). Physiological traits such as Relative Water Content (RWC), leaf rolling, chlorophyll content, and SPAD were measured. Morphological, phenological, and yield traits were also recorded.

Agro-morphological Characterization: Conducted using Row–Row design to evaluate landrace diversity in the field. Traits assessed included plant height, tiller number, panicle length, grain size, and qualitative descriptors as per IBPGR and UPOV standards.

Data Analysis: ANOVA, correlation analysis, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and cluster analysis were performed for both drought screening and characterization.
