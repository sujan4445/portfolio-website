Diversity Assessment and Drought Screening of Nepalese Rice Landraces

Evaluating drought tolerance and agro-morphological diversity in rice

Project Details

  • Funding Agency: Nepal Academy of Science and Technology (NAST)
  • Research Team: Sujan Chapagain, Sangita Chapagain, Itsa Bogati, Sonia K.C.
  • Supervisors: Ankur Poudel, Bishnu Prasad Kandel

Description

Nepalese rice landraces are adapted to diverse environments including drought-prone uplands. This project evaluates drought-resilient traits under controlled water stress while characterizing agro-morphological diversity to support conservation and breeding.

Background

Drought stress remains one of the major yield-limiting factors in rainfed rice systems in Nepal. Indigenous landraces contain valuable genes for climate resilience, yield stability, and grain quality.

Objectives

  • Screen landraces for drought tolerance in controlled environment.
  • Evaluate agro-morphological diversity under field conditions.
  • Identify promising genotypes for breeding and on-farm conservation.

Methods

Drought Screening: Conducted in Completely Randomized Design with two water regimes: well-watered (70% FC) and water-stressed (35% FC). Traits recorded included RWC, Chlorophyll fluorescence, leaf rolling, chlorophyll content (SPAD), morphological and yield attributes.

Field Characterization: Evaluated in Row–Row layout following IBPGR and UPOV descriptors for quantitative and qualitative traits.

Data Analysis: ANOVA, correlation, PCA, and cluster analysis.
