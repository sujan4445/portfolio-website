Background

Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is a fundamental staple cereal crop contributing significantly to food security, dietary energy supply, and rural livelihoods in Nepal. Most wheat research and varietal development have historically been concentrated in the Terai region, where irrigation facilities allow high-yielding varieties to thrive. However, the mid-hill region—comprising a large portion of the nation's cultivated area—remains predominantly rainfed and faces severe constraints due to erratic rainfall and terminal drought stress. This project addresses yield losses by systematically screening diverse local landraces, improved genotypes, and newly developed lines under controlled water regimes and field conditions.

Objectives

  • To assess the phenological, agronomic, and yield performance of local and improved wheat genotypes under contrasting irrigated and drought environments.
  • To screen for key morpho-physiological drought tolerance mechanisms including Relative Water Content (RWC), canopy temperature, SPAD chlorophyll index, and leaf rolling index.
  • To evaluate novel genotypes AFU W01 and AFU W02 alongside standard cultivars for yield stability across varied moisture regimes.
  • To provide actionable genetic and physiological data to support future wheat breeding programs and variety releases tailored for mid-hill ecosystems.

Outcomes

  • Identification of stable, high-yielding wheat lines resilient to moisture stress conditions in mid-hill rainfed environments.
  • Validation of physiological traits (RWC, chlorophyll stability, canopy depression) as effective indirect selection criteria for drought tolerance.
  • Characterization and baseline performance data for prospective lines AFU W01 and AFU W02.
  • Generation of multi-environment trial datasets supporting national breeding pipelines.
